Why the connection matters

The conditions of dictation

A long record,
carried by voice.

Nearly all of the Book of Mormon as published today was dictated between April 7 and June 30, 1829. Depending on how partial days are counted, historical reconstructions describe roughly 63–65 active working days—a matter of weeks for a narrative that spans centuries.

“When returning after meals, or after interruptions, he would at once begin where he had left off, without either seeing the manuscript or having any portion of it read to him.”

Emma Smith’s later recollection of serving as scribe

Emma also recalled that Joseph had “neither manuscript nor book to read from.” These are witness recollections; the working-day total is a historical estimate rather than a stopwatch measurement.
