01

Find the right research sourceGround a response in the correct SharePoint content.

Copilot Chat + SharePoint
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Practice selecting authoritative sources, separating current documents from superseded drafts, and asking Copilot to show its evidence.

Who it's for: Clinical and consumer researchers who work across shared research libraries
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
FICTIONAL PRACTICE SCENARIO
Persona: Jordan Lee, consumer insights researcher.
Practice date: November 9, 2026.

SharePoint library: Aurora Skin Comfort Study

1. /01_Governance/Aurora_Study_Charter_APPROVED_2026-10-12.docx
Status: Approved. Defines the study objective, population, decision owners, and allowed claims.

2. /02_Protocol/Aurora_Protocol_v3_APPROVED_2026-10-20.docx
Status: Current approved protocol. Primary endpoint: change in self-reported skin comfort after 14 days. Target sample: 120 participants.

3. /02_Protocol/Archive/Aurora_Protocol_v2_DRAFT_2026-10-04.docx
Status: Superseded draft. Target sample: 90 participants. Do not use for current study decisions.

4. /03_Fieldwork/Aurora_Fieldwork_Status_2026-11-06.xlsx
Status: Current operational snapshot. Enrolled: 84. Completed day 14: 52. Withdrawn: 4. Data cleaning has not started.

5. /04_Analysis/Aurora_Preliminary_Notes_2026-11-07.docx
Status: Analyst working notes, not quality checked. Contains an unsupported sentence: "The formula clearly improves comfort for all users."

6. /05_Readouts/Aurora_Final_Readout_TEMPLATE.pptx
Status: Blank template. No study results.

Library rule: Prefer approved and current documents. Treat operational snapshots as dated. Do not use superseded drafts, templates, or unverified analyst notes as final evidence.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesCreates a source map before asking for conclusions, reducing the chance that an old draft or unsupported note becomes evidence.
Goal: Tell me which files I should use to prepare an accurate status brief for the Aurora Skin Comfort Study.
Context: I am Jordan Lee, a consumer insights researcher preparing for a study checkpoint.
Source: Use only the pasted library map. Apply the library rule and cite each file by name.
Expectation: Classify each file as authoritative, useful with a date caveat, or exclude. Then give the approved sample target, current enrollment, completed day-14 count, and three unknowns. Do not infer efficacy.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesChecks whether Copilot can detect a conflict between an approved protocol and an older draft.
Which file contains the current sample target, and why should the 90-participant figure be excluded?
What this prompt doesTests whether Copilot distinguishes an unsupported conclusion from verified evidence.
Audit the sentence about improving comfort for all users. Explain exactly why it is unsupported and what evidence would be needed.
What to check▼
Expected evidence: The approved target is 120; 84 are enrolled; 52 completed day 14; 4 withdrew. The status is dated November 6, data cleaning has not started, and no efficacy conclusion is supported.
Tip: Name the source hierarchy▼
Tell Copilot which documents are approved, current, superseded, or unverified. A folder name alone does not establish authority.
Did you know? Copilot respects existing permissions▼
Microsoft states that Copilot applies the user's identity, permissions, sensitivity labels, retention policies, and administrative settings. Microsoft Learn
Recommended current-chat instructionEvidence before conclusions▼

Use this in the current chat. Do not save project names, study facts, participant information, or other sensitive material as memory.

Prefer approved and current sources. Label dated snapshots, exclude superseded drafts, and identify unsupported claims before drafting conclusions.

02

Turn a Teams discussion into a research checkpointSeparate observations, decisions, actions, and unresolved questions.

Teams
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Practice reviewing a meeting transcript without converting discussion into decisions that were never made.

Who it's for: Researchers, project leads, and managers who need reliable meeting follow-through
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
FICTIONAL PRACTICE TRANSCRIPT
Meeting: Aurora study checkpoint
Date: November 9, 2026
Participants: Jordan Lee (Research), Priya Shah (Analytics), Morgan Chen (Operations)

Jordan: We have 84 enrolled and 52 day-14 completions in the November 6 status file.
Morgan: Recruitment is slower than the original weekly plan. We may need another recruiting channel, but I have not confirmed feasibility or cost.
Priya: The preliminary notes should not be used for conclusions. Data cleaning has not started, and the primary endpoint has not been analyzed.
Jordan: Agreed. For Wednesday, let's bring a factual status and a list of decisions needed.
Morgan: I can ask the recruiting vendor for options and timing by November 10 at 3:00 PM Eastern Time.
Priya: I will draft the data-quality checklist by November 11 at noon Eastern Time.
Jordan: We still need someone to confirm whether the checkpoint date changes. No owner was assigned in this meeting.
Morgan: We did not approve a new recruiting channel today.
Jordan: Correct. We only agreed to gather options.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesProduces a structured recap while preserving the distinction between discussion, assigned actions, and unmade decisions.
Goal: Create a reliable checkpoint recap from this transcript.
Context: I am Jordan Lee and need a draft for the research team to review.
Source: Use only the pasted transcript. Quote or cite the speaker for decisions and commitments.
Expectation: Return four sections: factual status, decisions made, assigned actions with owner and due date, and unresolved questions. Explicitly state what was not approved. Draft only; do not send or assign anything.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesChallenges the recap for accidental overstatement.
List any statement in your recap that could be mistaken for an approved decision. Rewrite it more precisely.
What this prompt doesCreates a concise manager-ready version without losing uncertainty.
Condense the recap to 120 words while preserving every owner, due date, unknown, and non-decision.
What to check▼
Expected evidence: Morgan will request vendor options by November 10 at 3:00 PM ET; Priya will draft a checklist by November 11 at noon ET. No new channel or checkpoint-date change was approved, and no owner was assigned for the date decision.
Tip: Ask for a non-decision section▼
A recap is safer when it explicitly records proposals that were discussed but not approved.
Did you know? Post-meeting grounding depends on meeting capture▼
Microsoft documents that transcription or recording must be configured for Copilot to reference meeting content after a Teams meeting ends. Microsoft Learn
Recommended custom instructionMake meeting uncertainty visible▼

This is a stable working preference, not meeting data. Save it only if personalization is available and permitted by your organization.

When summarizing meetings, separate facts, decisions, proposed ideas, assigned actions, and unresolved questions. Never infer an owner or approval.

FICTIONAL PRACTICE EMAIL THREAD
Subject: Aurora recruiting options

From: Morgan Chen
Sent: November 9, 2026, 4:18 PM Eastern Time
To: Jordan Lee; Priya Shah
I asked the vendor for two options: extend the current channel by one week, or add a second channel. They will respond by November 10 at 3:00 PM ET. No cost or feasibility information is available yet.

From: Jordan Lee
Sent: November 9, 2026, 4:35 PM Eastern Time
To: Morgan Chen; Priya Shah
Thanks. Please do not interpret this as approval to change recruiting. We need the vendor response and an impact assessment first.

From: Priya Shah
Sent: November 9, 2026, 5:02 PM Eastern Time
To: Jordan Lee; Morgan Chen
For analysis integrity, I also need the proposed channel's screening method and whether it changes the participant mix. I can review those details after the vendor responds.

From: Morgan Chen
Sent: November 9, 2026, 5:20 PM Eastern Time

Understood. I will forward the vendor response when received. I have not committed to either option.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesFinds the latest state of the thread and drafts a response that keeps the decision open.
Goal: Summarize this email thread and draft my reply.
Context: I am Jordan Lee. The audience is Morgan and Priya, and the tone should be concise and collaborative.
Source: Use only the pasted email thread. Preserve timestamps and attribute commitments to the sender.
Expectation: First give the latest status, open evidence, and commitments. Then draft a reply of no more than 130 words that confirms next steps without approving either recruiting option. Draft only; do not send.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesChecks whether the draft accidentally creates a decision.
Audit the draft for language that could imply approval, urgency not present in the thread, or an owner who did not volunteer.
What this prompt doesAdapts the same evidence for a manager without restarting.
Create a three-bullet manager update: status, evidence needed, and next checkpoint. Keep the decision open.
What to check▼
Expected evidence: The vendor response is due November 10 at 3:00 PM ET. Cost, feasibility, screening method, and participant-mix impact are unknown. Morgan has not committed to either option.
Tip: Anchor on the latest message▼
Ask Copilot to preserve chronology and identify the newest commitment. Long threads often contain positions that later messages refine.
Did you know? Mailbox-grounded experiences need Exchange Online▼
Microsoft lists an Exchange Online mailbox as a requirement for experiences that use email, calendar, meeting, or other mailbox data. Microsoft Learn
Recommended current-chat instructionKeep correspondence draft-only▼

Use this when drafting a sensitive response. Review recipients, claims, attachments, and tone before sending.

Draft only. Preserve chronology, attribute commitments, identify missing evidence, and never turn a proposal into an approval.

04

Move from Excel evidence to a Word briefCalculate carefully, then write for a decision-maker.

Excel + Word
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Practice checking a small study table, stating calculation limits, and turning verified figures into a concise Word-ready brief.

Who it's for: Researchers and analysts who move between quantitative evidence and narrative readouts
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
FICTIONAL PRACTICE DATA
Aurora Skin Comfort Study operational snapshot
Snapshot date: November 6, 2026

Region | Enrolled | Day-14 completed | Withdrawn
North | 24 | 16 | 1
South | 22 | 13 | 2
East  | 20 | 12 | 1
West  | 18 | 11 | 0

Approved target sample: 120
Important limitations:
- These are operational counts, not efficacy results.
- Day-14 completion rate should use enrolled as the denominator unless the user specifies another denominator.
- Counts have not completed data cleaning or quality review.
- Regional differences must not be interpreted as product performance.
- No demographic or outcome data is included.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesRequests transparent arithmetic and a narrative that does not confuse operational progress with study outcomes.
Goal: Analyze this operational snapshot and prepare a short Word-ready status brief.
Context: I am a researcher updating a manager on recruitment and day-14 completion progress.
Source: Use only the pasted table and limitations. Show the arithmetic for totals and percentages.
Expectation: Calculate total enrolled, total completed, total withdrawn, enrollment versus target, and overall day-14 completion rate using enrolled as the denominator. Then write a 150-word brief with a headline, three bullets, limitations, and two questions. Do not claim efficacy or regional performance.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesTests boundary conditions in the calculations.
Recalculate each region's completion rate to one decimal place and verify that the regional counts sum to the totals. Flag any denominator ambiguity.
What this prompt doesTurns the checked analysis into a concise executive artifact.
Rewrite the brief for a senior research leader in 90 words. Keep the snapshot date and every limitation visible.
What to check▼
Expected arithmetic: 84 enrolled, 52 completed, 4 withdrawn, 70.0% of the 120 target enrolled, and 61.9% day-14 completion using 52 divided by 84. These figures are operational and uncleaned.
Tip: Ask Copilot to show its work▼
For calculations, require the formula, denominator, rounding rule, and a cross-check against the source totals.
Did you know? Embedded Copilot features require the licensed experience▼
Microsoft documents that the paid Microsoft Copilot license unlocks embedded Copilot features in Word, Excel, Outlook, and Teams, subject to app and tenant requirements. Microsoft Learn
Recommended custom instructionShow quantitative assumptions▼

This is a stable analytical preference. Do not save study values, participant details, or confidential thresholds.

For quantitative work, show formulas, denominators, rounding, snapshot dates, and limitations. Distinguish operational metrics from outcome evidence.

Copilot Chat
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Who it's for: Everyone who wants repeatable, reviewable results
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
FICTIONAL PRACTICE PROMPT

Original request:
"Review all our Aurora research and tell leadership whether the product works. Make it persuasive and email it today."

Available evidence in this exercise:
- Approved protocol with a 120-participant target and a day-14 self-reported comfort endpoint.
- November 6 operational counts: 84 enrolled, 52 day-14 completions, 4 withdrawals.
- A transcript stating data cleaning and endpoint analysis have not started.
- No outcome table, statistical analysis, safety review, approved claim language, leadership recipient list, or email authorization.

Task boundary:
The learner may draft a status brief and a list of evidence needed. The learner may not claim efficacy, approve language, identify recipients, or send an email.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesTransforms an overbroad, action-oriented prompt into a safe request that matches the available evidence.
Goal: Rewrite the original request as a strong GCSE prompt.
Context: I am preparing leadership for an Aurora study checkpoint, not a final efficacy decision.
Source: Use only the available-evidence list and task boundary in the pasted source.
Expectation: Produce one four-line GCSE prompt that requests a factual status brief, names the missing evidence, and stays draft-only. Then explain the five most important corrections you made.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesTests whether the repaired prompt still asks for unsupported claims.
Red-team the repaired prompt. Identify any wording that could still invite an efficacy claim, invented recipient, or unauthorized action.
What this prompt doesPractices iteration instead of restarting.
Revise only the expectation line so the output includes a claim-evidence-unknown table and a human-review checklist.
What to check▼
Expected repair: The new prompt should ask for status, not efficacy; name the limited sources; surface missing evidence; and say draft only. It must not ask Copilot to email anyone.
Tip: Iterate on one line▼
When the answer is close, revise the weakest GCSE line instead of replacing the whole prompt. This preserves useful context.

Open TXT
FICTIONAL RESEARCHER BRIEF
Project: Aurora skin comfort evidence scan
Requester: Jordan Lee, Consumer Insights
Decision date: November 18, 2026

Research question:
What evidence should the team examine before interpreting a 14-day self-reported skin-comfort endpoint, and which internal gaps prevent a conclusion today?

Internal facts available:
- Approved protocol: 120-participant target; primary endpoint is change in self-reported skin comfort after 14 days.
- November 6 snapshot: 84 enrolled; 52 completed day 14; 4 withdrawn.
- Data cleaning and endpoint analysis have not started.
- No outcome table, statistical analysis plan excerpt, adverse-event summary, subgroup plan, or approved claims language is available in this packet.

Requested public-source scope:
- Prefer peer-reviewed reviews, standards, regulator guidance, and primary research.
- Focus on patient/consumer-reported outcomes, short-duration cosmetic studies, missing data, measurement validity, and overgeneralization.
- Record publication date, source type, population, method, and limitations.
- Do not use public evidence to invent Aurora results.

Output boundary:
This is an evidence-planning exercise, not medical advice, regulatory approval, or a product claim.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesAsks Researcher to expose its plan, source strategy, and stopping conditions before synthesizing.
Goal: Create a research plan for the Aurora evidence scan.
Context: I am Jordan Lee preparing a decision-quality research brief for November 18, 2026.
Source: Use the pasted internal brief as the authoritative statement of Aurora facts. Use public web sources only for general methods and external evidence, with links and citations.
Expectation: Propose research subquestions, inclusion and exclusion criteria, a source hierarchy, search terms, an evidence table schema, and stopping conditions. Separate internal facts, public evidence, and unanswered Aurora questions. Do not draft a conclusion yet.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesTightens the plan around evidence quality.
Rank the planned source types from strongest to weakest for this question and explain what each can and cannot establish.
What this prompt doesCreates a checkpoint before Researcher spends time on a broad search.
Identify the three highest-risk ambiguities in my request and ask concise clarifying questions before continuing.
What to check▼
Expected plan: It should include measurement validity, missing data, population fit, duration, analysis readiness, and limitations. Aurora facts must remain separate from public methodological evidence.
Tip: Approve the plan before the synthesis▼
For a complex investigation, inspect the proposed questions and source hierarchy first. A polished answer cannot repair a poorly scoped search.
Did you know? Researcher is a licensed Microsoft Copilot capability▼
Microsoft lists Researcher among the additional capabilities available with the paid Microsoft Copilot license, subject to rollout and tenant controls. Microsoft Learn
Recommended current-chat instructionSeparate internal and public evidence▼

Use this for the investigation. Do not save project details or research questions as memory.

Keep internal facts, public evidence, analyst inference, and unanswered questions in separate labeled sections. Cite every material claim.

07

Audit a Researcher synthesisTrace claims back to evidence and expose gaps.

Researcher
What you'll practice · Who it's for▼
What it does

Practice reviewing citations, checking source fit, and turning uncertainty into next research actions.

Who it's for: Researchers and managers who must defend how a synthesis was produced
1. Paste the practice source▼
Paste this first - practice source
Open TXT
FICTIONAL DRAFT SYNTHESIS FOR AUDIT

Claim 1: "The Aurora study is on track because 84 participants are enrolled."
Support noted: November 6 operational snapshot.

Claim 2: "A 14-day self-reported endpoint is sufficient to prove durable product efficacy."
Support noted: No citation recorded.

Claim 3: "Four withdrawals demonstrate a low withdrawal rate."
Support noted: 4 withdrawals and 84 enrolled; no benchmark or reason-for-withdrawal data.

Claim 4: "The product improves skin comfort for all users."
Support noted: Preliminary analyst note; endpoint analysis has not started.

Claim 5: "Public literature can help assess measurement validity and common study limitations."
Support noted: Public methods literature, to be cited and evaluated for population and design fit.

Known internal constraints:
- Approved target is 120.
- 52 participants completed day 14.
- Data cleaning and endpoint analysis have not started.
- No subgroup results, adverse-event summary, statistical results, or approved claims language are in the packet.
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesTurns a plausible-looking synthesis into a claim-by-claim evidence audit.
Goal: Audit this draft synthesis for evidence quality and overstatement.
Context: I am reviewing a Researcher output before it is shared with research leadership.
Source: Use the pasted draft and constraints. If using public sources to explain a method, cite them, but do not use them to create Aurora results.
Expectation: Create a table with claim, evidence status, exact problem, corrected wording, and next evidence needed. Use Supported, Partially supported, or Unsupported. End with the three most important reviewer questions.
Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesChecks citation fit rather than citation presence.
For every public citation you used, explain whether its population, duration, outcome, and method fit the claim. Flag indirect evidence.
What this prompt doesProduces a short, defensible leadership summary.
Draft a 100-word evidence-status update that includes only supported internal facts and clearly labeled unknowns.
What to check▼
Expected audit: Claim 1 is only partial because enrollment alone does not establish schedule performance. Claims 2 and 4 are unsupported. Claim 3 lacks a benchmark and withdrawal reasons. Claim 5 is a valid methods use if citations fit.
Tip: Check whether a citation supports this claim▼
A real citation can still be the wrong evidence. Compare population, intervention, outcome, duration, and method before accepting it.
Did you know? Web search has distinct data-handling practices▼
Microsoft explains that web queries are generated from prompts, sent to Bing without user or tenant identifiers, and handled separately from Microsoft 365 data. Microsoft Learn
Recommended custom instructionAudit citation fit▼

This is a general research-quality preference and contains no study data.

For each material claim, show the supporting source and assess population, method, date, and limitation fit. Label indirect or missing evidence.

FICTIONAL AGENT CANDIDATES

A. Study Library Navigator
Answers recurring questions about approved protocols, templates, and study-status files in one governed SharePoint library. Must cite files and respect permissions. No actions.

B. Final Claims Approver
Reads draft study results and automatically approves consumer-facing product claims. Would require scientific, legal, regulatory, and brand authority.

C. Weekly Evidence Briefing Assistant
Creates a draft weekly summary from a defined SharePoint folder. Separates new documents, changed decisions, risks, and missing evidence. Human reviews before distribution.

D. One-Time Meeting Recap
Summarizes a single supplied transcript for one project checkpoint.

E. Participant Data Explorer
Answers unrestricted questions across row-level participant records, including sensitive data, without a defined access model or approved purpose.

Scoring criteria:
- Repeats often
- Has a stable audience and job
- Has a bounded, maintained knowledge source
- Can cite evidence
- Has clear human review
- Avoids authority it should not have
- Has an owner who can test and maintain it

Context: I am helping a research team choose one safe, high-value agent pilot.
Source: Use only the candidate descriptions and scoring criteria in the pasted source.

Follow-ups (2)▼
Follow-ups - same thread
What this prompt doesChallenges the recommended candidate for hidden risk.
Red-team the recommended pilot for overbroad access, stale sources, misleading citations, and authority creep.
What this prompt doesTurns the assessment into a concise stakeholder pitch.
Write a 90-second pilot pitch covering user, problem, source boundary, expected value, human review, and success measure.
What to check▼
Expected classification: A and C are plausible agent candidates; D is better as an ordinary Copilot prompt. B and E should not be built as described because authority and data boundaries are unacceptable.
Tip: Start with the job, not the technology▼
A good agent has a recurring job, bounded knowledge, a clear owner, and tests. A one-time request is usually just a prompt.

Before proposing an agent, test repeatability, source boundaries, citations, human review, authority, ownership, and maintenance.

Who it's for: Research knowledge owners and Copilot champions
1. Paste the practice source▼

Open TXT
FICTIONAL AGENT KNOWLEDGE INVENTORY
Agent name: GCCS Research Library Guide
Owner: Taylor Brooks, Research Knowledge Manager
Review cadence: Monthly, first business day

Allowed SharePoint source:
/GCCS-Training/Aurora-Approved-Library/

Included content:
- Approved study charters
- Current approved protocols
- Data dictionaries
- Standard research templates
- Quality-review checklists
- Final approved readouts

Excluded content:
- Archive folders
- Draft protocols
- Row-level participant data
- Unreviewed analyst notes
- Email and chat
- Personal OneDrive files

Agent job:
Help authorized users find and understand approved research materials. Cite the file and date. Distinguish policy, template, protocol, and study result.

Agent must not:
- Approve claims, studies, protocols, or communications
- Infer participant-level information
- Treat templates as completed evidence
- Answer from excluded or unavailable sources
- Send, publish, or change content

Required tests:
1. Normal question with an approved source
2. Conflicting approved and archived files
3. Missing-source question
4. Request for participant data
5. Request to approve a claim
6. Stale-document test after the monthly review date
Starting prompt (GCSE)▼
Starting prompt - Goal, Context, Source, Expectation
What this prompt doesCreates a complete, testable agent instruction set instead of relying on a broad description.
Goal: Draft the configuration for the GCCS Research Library Guide.
Context: I am Taylor Brooks, the fictional knowledge owner responsible for a safe pilot.
Source: Use only the pasted inventory. Do not assume access to any source not listed.
