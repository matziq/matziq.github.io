Domain 1 — Plan AI-powered business solutions

Domain 1 is 35–40% of AB-100, the largest share. It tests whether you can turn a business problem into the right kind of AI solution: what the task really is, whether the data is ready, how agents divide the work, and whether the investment pays off.

1.1 Analyze requirements for AI-powered business solutions

Before choosing a product, classify the business need. The study guide asks you to tell apart tasks that need generative AI, task automation, analytics, or decision support, and to judge whether the data behind them is fit for AI.

NeedWhat it looks likeTypical direction
Task automationRepeatable, rules-based steps across systems (approve, route, copy data)Power Automate, agent flows, Copilot Studio actions
Generative content / reasoningDrafting, summarizing, answering grounded questions, conversationMicrosoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Studio agents, Foundry models
AnalyticsTrends, KPIs, what happened and whyPower BI, Fabric, Copilot in analytics tools
Decision supportRecommendations a human approves (next best action, forecast)Dynamics 365 Copilot features, custom models, human-in-the-loop agents

Data readiness

Grounding quality caps answer quality. Assess data for being accurate, relevant, timely, clean, and available, and decide whether the same data should be reusable by other AI systems (for example, exposed once through Dataverse, Fabric, or a shared knowledge source rather than copied into each agent).

Exam trap

If the scenario says answers are wrong or outdated, the fix is usually the data or grounding (freshness, permissions, duplicates), not a bigger model.

1.2 Design an AI strategy and agent approach

The strategy objective is the heaviest single topic on this site’s blueprint. It mixes organizational adoption with technical design choices.

Adoption

  • Use the Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF) AI adoption guidance: strategy, plan, ready, govern, manage, secure.
  • An AI Center of Excellence sets standards, reusable assets, governance, and enablement so teams don’t reinvent agents.
  • Start with prioritized, measurable use cases; pilot, measure, scale.

Design choices

  • Extend Microsoft 365 Copilot when users already work in Microsoft 365 and need grounded productivity help.
  • Build in Copilot Studio for low-code custom agents with topics, actions, and connectors.
  • Build in Microsoft Foundry when you need pro-code control, custom or fine-tuned models, or complex orchestration.

Multi-agent solutions

Large processes are easier to secure, test, and evolve when split across focused agents. The study guide expects you to design multi-agent solutions that span Microsoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Studio, and Foundry.

  • Orchestrator (parent) agent routes the request; child or connected agents each own a narrow job and its tools.
  • Give each agent least-privilege access to only the data and actions it needs.
  • Define hand-offs, including when a human takes over.
  • Reuse agents across channels instead of duplicating logic.
Rule of thumb

Split when responsibilities, data permissions, or owners differ. Keep one agent when the task is small and shares one knowledge set.

Knowledge sources and grounding

Agents answer from knowledge sources such as SharePoint, Dataverse, websites, uploaded files, Microsoft Graph connectors, and Azure AI Search indexes. Plan which sources are authoritative, how they stay current, and how existing permissions are honored so users only see what they are allowed to see.

ConcernPlan for
AuthorityOne source of truth per fact; retire stale copies
FreshnessSync or index refresh cadence that matches how fast the data changes
SecurityPermission-trimmed retrieval; sensitivity labels; no oversharing
Retrieval qualityChunking, metadata, and search configuration (retrieval-augmented generation)

Prompt libraries, custom models, and small language models

  • Prompt libraries capture tested prompts so teams reuse what works and outputs stay consistent.
  • Prompt engineering first, then retrieval grounding, then fine-tuning only when behavior or format still falls short.
  • Small language models (SLMs) fit narrow, high-volume, low-latency, cost-sensitive, or on-device scenarios.
  • Custom models in Foundry fit specialized domains or tasks a general model handles poorly.

1.3 Evaluate costs, benefits, and ROI

Architects must justify the solution. Build an ROI and total cost of ownership (TCO) view that includes more than licenses.

Costs

  • Licensing and consumption (messages, tokens, capacity)
  • Build, integration, and data preparation
  • Ongoing monitoring, tuning, and support
  • Governance, security, and change management

Benefits

  • Time saved and throughput gained
  • Quality, accuracy, and customer satisfaction
  • Revenue impact and risk reduction
  • Measured against a baseline captured before rollout

Build, buy, or extend — and model routing

ChoicePick it when
Buy (prebuilt agent / Dynamics 365 Copilot feature)The scenario is common and a Microsoft capability already covers it; fastest value, least maintenance
Extend (Microsoft 365 Copilot agents, connectors, plugins)Users live in Microsoft 365 and need their business data or actions inside Copilot
Build (Copilot Studio or Foundry)The process is differentiating or needs custom logic, models, or orchestration

A model router sends each request to the most suitable model, balancing quality, latency, and cost — simple requests go to cheaper models, hard ones to more capable models.

Cheat sheet

Classify first

Automation, generative, analytics, or decision support decides the tool.

Data before models

Accurate, relevant, timely, clean, available, reusable.

Cheapest fit wins

Buy before extend before build, unless the process differentiates you.
