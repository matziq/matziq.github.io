The scoring trap that costs people points

Passing is 700 on a scaled 1–1000 range. That is not the same as 70% correct. Microsoft says a passing score of 700 may not equal 70% of the points, some questions are unscored, and the raw-to-scaled conversion is not published.

So this site never estimates a scaled score. Practice results are shown as percent correct, with this site’s own study target of 80% as a safety margin. Use the domain breakdown to decide what to fix next.

The useful non-facts

Microsoft does not publish AB-100’s question count, a single global price, or the raw-to-scaled formula. If a source gives you any of those as fact, treat it as a guess.

Retakes and renewal

Retake policy

  • After a first failure, wait 24 hours before retaking.
  • After later failures, wait 14 days between attempts.
  • Maximum five attempts in a 12-month period.
  • You cannot retake an exam you passed unless the certification has expired.
  • Each retake is paid.

Microsoft retake policy

Renewal

Expert certifications expire after one year. Renew for free by passing an online, unproctored, open-book renewal assessment on Microsoft Learn during the six-month window before expiration.

35–40%. The largest domain.

Exam-day focus

Verify the current Microsoft Learn exam page before you sit the exam. Confirm the exam code, appointment time, the exam length shown at registration, and your ID requirements.

During role-based exams Microsoft lets you open Microsoft Learn in a restricted window, but the clock keeps running — it is a safety net, not a study strategy. Breaks, if offered, also run the clock, and you cannot return to earlier questions after one.

Read every scenario for the constraint that decides it: lowest effort, least privilege, no-code vs pro-code, data residency, or cost.
