Arizona school rules, not park-league rules.

AIA Article 25.1.1 adopts the current NFHS Flag Football Rules Book. Arizona's published state adoptions also apply. This guide summarizes the public AIA 2026–27 bylaws, the AIA timing guide, and NFHS 2026–27 changes and points of emphasis. It is not the official rulebook or an AIA/school publication. [A1, A2, N1, N2]

Important scope: This is the AIA high school girls' game. NFL FLAG, club leagues, Unified flag football, and other states can use different rules. The school's current rulebook, AIA interpretations, and game officials resolve situations this practical summary does not cover.

The NFHS first published its flag rulebook in 2025–26; 2026–27 is an updated edition. In particular, do not reuse last year's two-timeout rule, assume that a fallen belt automatically ends a play, or borrow a recreational seven-yard rush rule without checking the actual code.

The game at a glance.

Verified essentials from AIA and NFHS public materials

PlayersSeven against seven. The 2026–27 NFHS change specifically requires seven players to start a game. [N1]
FieldArizona permits 360 × 120 feet or 300 × 120 feet: 120 × 40 or 100 × 40 yards overall. Each includes two 10-yard end zones, leaving 100 or 80 yards between goal lines. Team boxes are between the 25-yard lines; goalposts are required. [A1 §25.1.3, N3]
First downsFour downs to reach the next designated zone line, not automatically 10 yards. NFHS establishes the zone to gain at the next applicable 20- or 40-yard line ahead when a series begins; the goal line is the final target. On the 100-yard playing field, these are own 20, own 40, opponent 40, opponent 20, then goal. Use the marked zones for the shorter field. [A2, N4]
ExampleStarting on your own 14: the first target is your 20, only 6 yards away. A new series at your 23 targets your 40, 17 yards away. Penalties and changes of possession can alter the situation; listen to the official's down and zone announcement.
Game lengthFour 12-minute periods, with AIA running-clock procedures and listed stoppages. Standard halftime is 10 minutes; special ceremonies may extend it to no more than 20. [A1 §25.1.5, A2]
Start / restartAIA's timing guide lists a new series at the offense's 14 to begin each half and after scoring. Exception: the 2026–27 national change requires the team whose goal line was involved in a safety to scrimmage-kick from its own 20. The new trailing-team possession option also needs current-rulebook administration. [A2, N1]
Flag, not bodyEnd a normal run by removing the ball carrier's flag or flag belt. No tackling, wrapping up, pushing down, charging through opponents, or stripping a possessed ball. Non-contact screening is allowed when legal. [N1, N2]

Clock, points, and a tied game.

Keep track of time

  • Three charged timeouts per team per half. Unused timeouts do not carry to the next half or overtime. [N1, A2]
  • 25-second play clock from the referee's ready-for-play signal; the referee handles it if there is no visible clock. [A2]
  • The clock stops for penalties, official timeouts, charged team/TV timeouts, possession changes, touchdowns, and safeties. Penalty/official-timeout restarts follow the referee's signal; the other listed restarts are on the snap. It does not run during the try. [A2]
  • A two-minute timeout occurs in the second and fourth quarters. Inside the last two minutes of a half, incompletions and out-of-bounds plays stop the clock, restarting on the snap. First downs also stop it, with restart on the referee's signal. [A2]

Mercy rule

Once a team leads by at least 28 points, the clock runs except for team, official, or injury timeouts. Once activated, this rule remains in effect for the rest of the contest even if the margin shrinks. [A1 §25.1.7]

Varsity overtime

Each team receives a first-and-goal series from the opponent's 10-yard line at the same end of the field. The initial coin toss allocates offense/defense order or field-end choice. If tied after both series, repeat with a two-minute intermission; first choice alternates without another toss. Each team gets one timeout per overtime period, with no carryover. A defensive gain of possession immediately ends that down and offensive series: no overtime interception return for a score. The goal line remains the line to gain despite penalty enforcement. [A1 §25.1.6]

Double-teamed? Win the space, not the collision.

A legal screen uses position. An illegal block uses contact.

NFHS's 2026–27 emphasis is explicit: flag football is non-contact. Intentional contact by offense or defense is a foul. Two blockers are not automatically illegal; each must establish and maintain a legal screen. [N2]

A blocker must

  • Establish position before the defender arrives, without leaning into or initiating contact.
  • Leave a reasonable chance to avoid the screen, generally one to two steps, judged in context.
  • Keep arms at the sides, across the chest, or behind the back. Do not extend elbows, forearms, shoulders, knees, or legs to obstruct.
  • Once established, move only in the opponent's same direction and path while maintaining the legal frame and avoidance opportunity.

Alina must

  • Avoid a screen that was legally established.
  • Go around rather than push, pull, hand-fight, shoulder through, or force a closed gap.
  • Adjust early; a legal screen is not permission to make contact just because the QB is behind it.
  • Tell the coach if a blocker repeatedly steps into her at the last moment or extends an arm. Do not retaliate.

The legal pass-rush toolbox.

These are non-contact coaching suggestions consistent with the screening emphasis, not named moves guaranteed legal in every situation. Legality depends on actual movement and contact. Have the coach teach them at walking speed first. [N2]

ToolHow it helpsBoundary
One-step jukeShow one short step toward one side, then accelerate into an open lane.Plant under control; no shoulder check, arm swipe, or collision with the screen.
Change of paceBriefly shorten the approach, let a screen show its position, then burst toward free space.Do not stop so close that the only next move is through the blocker.
Wide-to-tight arcUse the open edge, then curve toward the QB's hip once clear.Stay in the contain assignment; do not sprint far past the QB and open a scramble lane.
Single counterIf the first lane closes, redirect once to a clearly open lane.No blind spin into people or repeated dancing that loses leverage.
Disciplined pressureMake the QB hold the ball, move, or throw early while teammates cover.A useful rep does not have to end in a sack. Do not abandon the scheme chasing statistics.

Leave tackle-football moves out: no bull rush, stiff-arm, rip through an opponent, swim with arm contact, club, jersey grab, or shove. At the quarterback, play the flag. Hitting the passer's arm is roughing the passer; do not swat the ball while it is held. [N1, N2]

When doubled: identify the contain job, locate the nearest open edge, use one convincing footwork fake, and stay balanced approaching the QB. If both screens seal the available space, contain and communicate; the correct answer is not to force contact. Ask the coach about a coordinated rush adjustment rather than freelancing.

Flag pulling is the tackle. No body tackle needed.

Legal finish

Track the runner's hips, close under control, and make a clear attempt to remove a flag. Slight incidental contact may occur during a legitimate pull; this does not authorize pushing, holding, tripping, wrapping, or tackling. Contact without a flag-pull attempt is a foul. When a flag or belt is removed from a player possessing the ball, the ball is dead. Stop at the whistle and return the equipment respectfully. [N2]

The easily missed exceptions

  • Belt falls off by itself: play is not automatically dead. The defense ends it with a one-hand touch between shoulders and knees, including arms and hands. This is a light touch, not a shove. [N2, Rule 4-2-2c]
  • Flag pulled during a bobble: there was no possession yet. After the receiver gains possession, a defender must make the required one-hand touch to end the play. [N2]
  • Flag guarding: using a hand, arm, or the ball with contact that denies a flag-pull opportunity is a foul. NFHS's current emphasis says contact is required for this specific foul. It is a 10-yard penalty. Other dangerous or illegal actions can still be fouls without meeting this exact definition. [N2, Rule 9-2-2c]
  • After a touchdown: a teammate removes the scorer's flag in the official's view before celebrating, demonstrating that it was pullable. Tampering can negate the touchdown and eject the head coach. [N2]

Equipment check before every session

Arizona requires a one-piece quick-release Triple Threat belt with a spring-loaded clip. Each flag must be at least 2 inches wide and 14 inches long, measured from the belt edge. This Arizona width exceeds the national 1.5-inch minimum. NFHS calls for three flags: one at each hip and one at the tailbone. Flags must be a solid color contrasting clearly with shorts/pants; no clothing or hair may cover the belt or flags, and no manufacturer or school logos belong on them. Do not knot, tape, alter, or otherwise defeat the release. [A1 §25.1.4, N2]

Wear the team's required mouthguard and approved footwear and uniform; have the coach inspect jewelry, pockets, and any braces or protective equipment against the current equipment rules. Practice with the actual competition belt rather than a different pull-off system.

Receiver awareness and possession.

Alina's receiver practice should combine secure catches with correct alignment, timing, spacing, and the coach's route landmarks. Route-running footwork can create separation; pushing off, grabbing, or deliberately screening a defender with an illegal pick cannot. Do not confuse avoiding a defender with being allowed to initiate contact. [N2]

Useful playing habits

  • Get the formation and snap count from the coach. Check your alignment with an official when appropriate.
  • Release into open space without contacting the defender. A head/shoulder fake is a movement cue, not permission to bump.
  • Catch first, secure the ball, then turn. Practice clear inbounds control with both feet whenever possible.
  • Know the zone to gain and the sideline. A catch short of the target is not necessarily a first down.
  • After the catch, use cuts and changes of pace with accessible flags. No stiff-arms or contacting a defender with the ball to deny a pull.

Dead-ball discipline

Stop for the whistle. Do not dive into a loose-ball pile, try to strip a runner, or tackle a player whose flags are hard to reach.

Flag pulls, a fallen belt, a bobbling catch, an incomplete pass, an interception, and a kick touching the ground are different situations. The flag exceptions above and kick rules below are especially important.

Confirm procedural details: this guide does not replace the current rulebook's catch definition, grounded-ball/fumble provisions, legal forward-pass requirements, motion/formation rules, or QB-running restrictions. Do not import an NFL catch rule or a recreational no-run-zone rule.

Jukes versus jumps: a controlled cut or change of pace is the safer tool to train. NFHS added a hurdling definition for 2026–27 and already treated hurdling as an illegal personal-contact foul. Do not practice hurdling an opponent or diving through players to gain yards. Jumping to catch a pass is a different situation; confirm the relevant rule with the coach. [N1]

Kicks and the 2026 details worth knowing.

Arizona allows field goals and kick tries. Do not assume all flag football eliminates kicking. [A1 §25.1.8]

Declared scrimmage kicks

The kicking team declares its intention. It must then kick rather than fake a normal play unless a charged timeout, official timeout, end of period, or penalty enforcement permits a new declaration. The receiving team gets substitution time. Before the kick, the kicking team is set and stationary; movement is a false start. The receiver of the snap must kick within five seconds, or it is a 5-yard delay penalty. [N2]

The receiving team may catch an airborne kick or recover an untouched grounded kick and return it. Once any player touches the kick and it subsequently hits the ground, it is dead and belongs to the receiving team at that spot. A scrimmage kick breaking the receiving team's goal-line plane is a touchback, subject to the first-touching choice. First-touching and a kick touched by the receivers then caught by the kicking team have additional provisions; let the official administer them. [N2]

What changed for 2026–27?

  • Teams must start with seven players; timeouts increased from two to three per half.
  • Following a safety, the team whose goal line was involved scrimmage-kicks from its own 20.
  • A new high-risk possession-retention option is available to a team trailing after scoring. The public change notice does not state its full distance and administration; use the current rulebook rather than guessing.
  • A fourth national field option and state-postseason instant replay were added as state-adoption options. They are not automatic Arizona adoptions; Article 25 lists Arizona's two field dimensions.
  • Hurdling now has an explicit definition, and the prohibition on actions against a ball in player possession was clarified. [N1]

Five sideline situations

Two blockers are standing in Alina's path. Is that automatically a foul?

No. Judge each screen's position, timing, movement, and avoidance space. Alina must avoid legal screens; she cannot push through simply because there are two. [N2]

A blocker shuffles across and bumps Alina at the last instant.

Potential illegal screen. A blocker cannot remove her reasonable opportunity to avoid the screen or initiate contact. Continue safely, and have the coach address it with officials rather than retaliating. [N2]

The receiver's belt falls off without a defender touching it.

Do not stop just because the belt fell. A legal one-hand touch between shoulders and knees, including arms/hands, is required to end this play. [N2]

A runner moves her arm near a flag, but makes no contact.

Not flag guarding solely on that description. The current NFHS emphasis requires contact that denies the flag-pull opportunity for that specific foul. Different conduct may violate a different rule. [N2]

Alina cannot get a sack but keeps the QB from escaping outside.

That can be a good defensive rep. Containment and pressure can help teammates finish the play. This is coaching guidance, not a scoring rule; ask how her coach grades her assignment.

A two-minute check with the coach.

Resolve these details from the 2026–27 NFHS book and current AIA instructions before practicing game-speed situations. Public summaries are not enough for every technical ruling.

  • Confirm Alina's rush alignment, neutral-zone spacing, snap trigger, and contain responsibility; do not assume a seven-yard rush rule.
  • Demonstrate a legal moving screen, including time and distance, and a legal response to a double screen.
  • Confirm formation/motion requirements, pass eligibility, legal forward-pass location, QB runs, catch definition, and grounded-ball rules.
  • Confirm kick administration, the current trailing-team possession option, and the safety restart.
  • Inspect her competition belt, flag placement, uniform, mouthguard, hair, and other equipment.
  • Confirm field-specific zone markings, current clock instructions, and any newer AIA interpretations.
