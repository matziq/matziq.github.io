How to study this exam

AB-100 tests architecture judgment across business applications and AI platforms. Study each objective as a sequence: identify the business outcome, inspect the data and constraints, choose the least-complex adequate capability, define the controls and operating model, and state how success will be measured. A product name without a reasoned fit is rarely a complete design.

  1. Start from the exam outline. Convert each verb into evidence you can produce: assess, design, recommend, validate, monitor, or secure. Track gaps by objective group rather than by product alone.
  2. Practice trade-offs. For each scenario, compare a built-in copilot, Microsoft 365 Copilot extensibility, Copilot Studio, and a custom Foundry implementation. Explain what you gain and what new work you own.
  3. Draw the end-to-end path. Map identity, data access, grounding, orchestration, tools, human approval, telemetry, release, and rollback. A solution is not complete if its runtime cannot be governed.
  4. Use Learn to confirm product behavior. Pages and feature availability change. Distinguish documented GA behavior from preview, region, licensing, or tenant-specific constraints.
  5. Review failure cases. Test stale or conflicting records, missing permissions, low-confidence retrieval, prompt injection, tool failure, and duplicate or irreversible actions.

Exam tip
When two options both sound technically possible, prefer the one that satisfies the stated business requirement with the smallest custom surface area while preserving security, manageability, and measurable outcomes.

Use the practice assessments and exam sandbox linked from Microsoft's exam resources, but do not treat a practice score as a guarantee of the scaled exam score. For each missed item, record the objective, the missed decision, the source that corrected it, and one rule you can reuse.

Plan AI-powered business solutions
1.1 Analyze requirements

Begin with the work, not the agent. Classify the candidate activity as task automation (a repeatable sequence with bounded inputs and outputs), data analytics (summarizing, finding patterns, or answering questions over governed information), or decision support (presenting evidence and options to an accountable person). An agent is strongest when it can interpret variable requests and coordinate approved tools; deterministic workflow logic is often better for fixed, auditable transitions. High-impact decisions need explicit human accountability and a way to challenge or override the result.

For grounding, assess the information at the point it will be used. Accuracy asks whether values match the authoritative source; relevance asks whether the content answers this task; timeliness asks whether it is current enough; cleanliness covers duplicates, malformed fields, and inconsistent terminology; availability covers access, indexing, permissions, and service dependencies. A model cannot repair a missing permission or turn a stale policy into current truth. Record owner, source-of-truth, update cadence, sensitivity, retention, and quality thresholds for each dataset.

QuestionEvidence to collectArchitecture consequence
Does the work vary by case?Exception rate, intents, decision points, escalation patternsUse rules/flows for stable branches; use an agent for language-driven variation within a defined policy envelope.
Can data support a grounded answer?Source owner, freshness, permission model, relevance and quality samplesRepair data or access first; then select an authorized retrieval path and test citations/coverage.
What is the impact of an error?Financial, customer, safety, legal, and reputational consequencesIncrease review, validation, auditability, and limits on action as impact rises.
Must other AI systems reuse the data?Stable identifiers, schemas, API or connector contracts, metadata, access modelPublish governed interfaces and common semantics instead of copying data into an isolated agent.

Architect scenario. Fabrikam's service team wants an agent to answer warranty questions and initiate replacements. Product manuals are current, but serial-number records are duplicated across an ERP export and CRM. Separate read-only question answering from replacement initiation; establish the authoritative serial source and identity/permission behavior before exposing the action. Require confirmation and a traceable transaction result for a replacement rather than allowing a fluent answer to imply that the action succeeded.

Lab 1.1 — Grounding readiness brief
  1. Choose a fictional process and list its inputs, decisions, outputs, exception paths, and accountable owner.
  2. Inventory three grounding sources; score accuracy, relevance, timeliness, cleanliness, and availability with evidence, not guesses.
  3. Mark sensitive fields, source-of-truth, allowed readers, update cadence, and what happens when a source is unavailable.
  4. Sketch the agent's read and write boundaries, including a human checkpoint for consequential actions.

Success check: another architect can identify the authoritative evidence for each answer, explain what the agent may change, and name a measurable data-quality gate.

Exam tip
Separate a content-quality problem from a model problem. If the grounding corpus is stale, incomplete, or inaccessible, switching models is not the first fix.

Did you know?

The AB-100 outline explicitly calls out grounding data's accuracy, relevance, timeliness, cleanliness, and availability. Treat these as distinct checks; a corpus can score well on one and fail another.

1.2 Design an overall AI strategy

Translate strategy into a portfolio, not a collection of demos. The Cloud Adoption Framework's AI guidance starts from business value and distinguishes productivity copilots, in-product copilots, role-aligned assistants, custom agents, and development platforms. Match the capability to the job and required control: use an existing product capability when it addresses the need; extend Microsoft 365 Copilot when users need domain-specific skills in that work surface; use Copilot Studio for configurable business agents and process integration; consider Foundry when the solution needs developer control over models, tools, hosting, or custom orchestration.

A multi-agent design should have a reason for each agent boundary. Delegate to specialized agents when they own bounded expertise, tools, or permissions; retain a clear orchestrator that routes work, handles ambiguity, and applies policy. Define the contract between agents (input, output, identity, authorization, error state, and provenance). Avoid splitting one straightforward task into agents simply because the platform supports it. Establish prompt-library governance—owner, intended scenario, approved context, version, evaluation set, and retirement path—and consider a tuned/custom model only when evidence shows that configuration, retrieval, or model selection cannot meet the requirement.

Strategic choicePrefer whenValidate before committing
Prebuilt/product copilotThe business task aligns with supported functionality and built-in context.Current capability, configuration, data permissions, licensing, and process fit.
Extend Microsoft 365 CopilotUsers need domain actions or knowledge inside Microsoft 365 experiences.Connector/agent capability, user identity, data boundary, governance and user experience.
Copilot Studio agentLow-code authoring, business orchestration, channels, and Power Platform integration fit.Knowledge and tool contracts, policy controls, channel needs, lifecycle and telemetry.
Custom Foundry solutionCustom model/tool behavior, code-level orchestration, or runtime control is essential.Engineering, operations, evaluation, security, cost, hosting and support burden.
Custom/tuned modelA documented task-specific gap remains after prompt, retrieval, and model evaluation.Representative data rights, quality, fairness, maintenance, validation and rollback.

Include the operating model in the strategy: an AI Center of Excellence or equivalent should set reusable standards, risk tiers, ownership, platform guardrails, evaluation and adoption measures, escalation paths, and learning loops. These structures guide teams; they do not replace process owners, security review, or product-specific governance. For solutions spanning Dynamics 365 apps, map shared customer/process context and each app's system of record rather than assuming a single copilot sees every record.

Architect scenario. Northwind wants a sales assistant in Outlook and a warranty agent in its service portal, with shared product knowledge and different write permissions. Reuse an approved knowledge source and common identity policy, but keep the channel-specific agents and tools bounded. Use a central orchestration design only if there are multi-step cross-domain tasks that justify the added operational coupling.

Lab 1.2 — Strategy and agent boundary map
  1. Map three use cases to an existing copilot, Microsoft 365 extensibility, Copilot Studio, or Foundry; state why alternatives are not preferred.
  2. Draw a multi-agent design only where separate expertise or permission boundaries add value. Label orchestrator, agents, data, tools, identity, and handoffs.
  3. Draft a prompt-library entry with owner, version, scenario, allowed grounding, expected behavior, test cases, and retirement trigger.
  4. List the AI Center of Excellence responsibilities and the business, security, and platform owners who approve releases.

Success check: the design has a business owner, a justified build-versus-extend decision, explicit boundaries, and a way to test each agent's role.

Exam tip
Use custom models to close a proven capability gap—not as the default response to a requirement for domain knowledge. Retrieval, instructions, tool design, or an available model may meet the need with less lifecycle risk.

1.3 Evaluate costs and benefits

Evaluate an AI investment against a baseline process. Define who performs the work, current volume, cycle time, quality, rework, exception handling, and cost per case. Estimate benefits only for changes that the solution can plausibly cause—such as reduced handling time, fewer errors, faster resolution, or increased capacity—and state adoption, deflection, escalation, and realization assumptions. Distinguish cash savings from released capacity; capacity is not automatically a budget reduction.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) includes more than model usage: discovery and integration, data preparation, licenses and consumption, platform operations, security/compliance review, evaluation, monitoring, human review, change management, support, and refresh or retirement. Avoid hard-coded pricing in an architecture estimate; prices, entitlements, consumption meters, and regional availability vary. Use the current licensing/pricing tools and a sensitivity analysis instead.

DimensionMeasure or estimateDecision use
BaselineVolume, minutes per case, error/rework rate, wait time, fully loaded labor assumptionEstablish a comparable counterfactual and disclose data limitations.
BenefitNet minutes saved, quality improvement, throughput, customer outcomes, avoided workApply adoption and eligible-case rates; avoid attributing unrelated changes to AI.
TCOBuild/buy/extend, integration, data, licenses/consumption, controls, operations, changeCompare full lifecycle and marginal costs at expected scale.
Risk and uncertaintyQuality range, fallbacks, review effort, model changes, adoption sensitivityUse conservative/base/upside cases and identify the assumptions that could reverse a decision.

Build when differentiated capability or control warrants owning the engineering and maintenance. Buy or use a prebuilt capability when it meets the requirement with less total effort and acceptable controls. Extend when an existing platform or copilot already provides the user experience and governance base. A model router can direct requests to an eligible model according to routing settings, balancing quality, cost, and latency; it does not eliminate evaluation. Compare it with a baseline for your workload, account for eligible model access and context requirements, and monitor outcome quality as model pools change.

Architect scenario. Litware proposes routing every request to its most capable model. First segment request types, establish quality and latency thresholds, and estimate volume by task. Test a router or smaller model against the baseline. Route only where evaluation demonstrates acceptable quality and cost/latency benefit; retain a fallback for unsupported requests and monitor costs and outcomes.

Lab 1.3 — ROI and TCO model
  1. Document the baseline monthly volume, time, error/rework rate, and cost assumptions for one business process.
  2. Estimate eligible cases, adoption, net minutes saved, quality change, and ongoing human review. Keep cash savings separate from capacity released.
  3. Build a three-year TCO with implementation, data, licensing/consumption, operations, governance, change, and contingency categories; use current quotes rather than invented unit prices.
  4. Compare build, buy, extend, and an evaluated model-routing option under conservative, base, and upside assumptions.

Success check: every benefit and cost has an assumption, owner, source, and sensitivity; the recommendation states a measurable pilot gate and stop/scale criteria.

Exam tip
When the prompt asks for ROI, include the counterfactual and full operating cost. A percentage productivity claim without adoption, quality, or human-review assumptions is not an ROI analysis.

Design AI-powered business solutions
2.1 Design AI and agents

Choose an interaction pattern based on uncertainty, authority, and action risk. A prompt-and-response interaction responds to an explicit request; a task agent helps complete a bounded job with tools and possibly human approval; an autonomous agent can act when a trigger or objective occurs without a person prompting each step. Autonomy is a business and risk choice, not a synonym for sophistication. Define triggers, permitted actions, thresholds, retry behavior, approval gates, audit events, and a stop mechanism before increasing autonomy.

For Copilot Studio, distinguish deterministic topic/NLP handling from conversational language understanding (CLU) and generative orchestration. Use predictable topic/rule paths when precise, repeatable handling is important; CLU when intent classification and entity extraction over known utterance patterns are central; generative orchestration when natural-language requests need flexible planning across reusable tools, topics, knowledge, and agents. Match the approach to the variability, determinism, latency, explainability, and testability required, and validate the current capabilities of the target harness.

PatternUseful whenDesign control
Rules / classic topicKnown intents and mandatory sequence require deterministic routing.Explicit fallback, slot validation, and tested branches.
CLURecognize a bounded set of intents/entities from varied utterances.Representative utterance coverage, confidence handling, and out-of-scope fallback.
Generative orchestrationRequests vary and a planner should select from approved tools and knowledge.Tool descriptions, least privilege, policy boundaries, plan evaluation, and safe escalation.
Prompt agent / responseInstructions, model, and tools can satisfy the experience with managed execution.Grounding, output validation, tool authorization, and clear user expectation.
Custom Foundry ToolA specific capability such as speech, search, or model inference is needed.Fit tool to requirement; verify data flow, deployment, and integration constraints.

In Dynamics 365 customer-experience and service scenarios, map the business terms and actions to the actual app, records, roles, channel, and user permissions. Sales capabilities may include contextual summaries and assistance, but availability and configuration vary. For Contact Center, define the channel and handoff to a human agent as part of the solution rather than treating the bot as a separate front end. In Power Apps canvas processes, place AI prompts or agents at a useful decision point, validate inputs and outputs, and preserve the ordinary process path when AI is unavailable.

Use a prompt action for a bounded transformation or generation step; make its inputs explicit and validate its output before using it downstream. A Foundry Tool should be proposed because its capability meets a stated requirement—not because a particular product name appears on a list. Power Platform Well-Architected thinking applies reliability, security, operational excellence, performance efficiency, and experience considerations to the whole workload, including its non-AI dependencies.

Architect scenario. Woodgrove wants to triage support messages and autonomously issue credits for late deliveries. Design generative routing for varied messages, but keep credit issuance behind policy validation and a human or deterministic approval threshold. A no-match, low-confidence, stale-order, or unavailable-CRM path should route to a person with the evidence gathered.

Lab 2.1 — Agent pattern decision record
  1. Write one user journey with normal, ambiguous, out-of-scope, and tool-failure paths.
  2. Choose rules/topic, CLU, generative orchestration, prompt agent, or custom tool for each step; justify fit in terms of variability and determinism.
  3. Mark which steps are read-only, reversible, or consequential; define approval, validation, and fallback for each write.
  4. For one Power Apps or Dynamics process, show where the AI component appears and how the normal process continues if it fails.

Success check: a reviewer can trace a request to its source data, selected tool, authorization decision, user confirmation, and final outcome.

Exam tip
Do not equate autonomous with unbounded. The strongest design usually automates low-risk steps and limits high-impact actions with policy checks, confirmations, or human review.

2.2 Design extensibility

Extensibility is a contract problem: establish what the agent can know, what it can do, whose identity it uses, and how the host controls that interaction. Microsoft 365 Copilot extensibility is appropriate when an agent or capability should participate in a Microsoft 365 work experience. Copilot Studio offers a low-code route to configure agent behavior and connect tools/knowledge; Foundry supports developer-oriented model and agent approaches, including managed prompt or hosted agents. Choose based on required user experience, control, integration, and lifecycle—not a blanket preference for no-code or code.

Model Context Protocol (MCP) standardizes how clients discover and call tools exposed by a server. It is not a permission grant: configure authentication, authorization, tool scope, input validation, and data handling independently. For a custom model in Foundry, document the gap it addresses, model/data provenance, evaluation set, deployment and version strategy, and rollback. Configure agent behaviors such as reasoning and voice only when they help the task and can be tested for quality and safety. For Teams and SharePoint, map the information boundary and existing access controls that determine what users can retrieve.

Extension choiceArchitect forBoundary to prove
Microsoft 365 Copilot agentWork-context interaction in supported Microsoft 365 experiences.Identity, data source, permission trimming, discoverability, and tenant governance.
Copilot Studio tools / connectorsBusiness agent actions using managed connectors, APIs, or flows.Connection identity, least privilege, DLP, parameter validation, and failure behavior.
MCP tool serverReusable, described tool interface for compatible clients/agents.Server trust, authentication, exposed-tool allowlist, output handling, and versioning.
Computer useUI interaction when no suitable API or structured integration meets the need.Allowed apps/sites, user confirmation, environment isolation, visual-state errors, and audit.
Custom model / hosted agentSpecific capability or code-level control that managed configuration cannot meet.Model validation, application ownership, secrets, patching, observability, and release process.

Prefer APIs and structured connectors for stable business operations because their contracts can be validated and governed. Computer use can be a fit where a UI is the only practical interaction surface, but it is sensitive to interface changes and should be tested for misclicks, unexpected prompts, and confirmation requirements. A hosted agent provides control over code and framework, but also makes the team responsible for application-level lifecycle and operations; a prompt agent delegates more runtime management to Foundry.

Architect scenario. Adatum must let an internal agent look up a case and draft an update, while only a case owner may submit it. Expose a read tool and a separately authorized write tool; require confirmation and enforce authorization in the business system. A shared MCP endpoint may make the tool reusable, but it must not make all callers inherit the most privileged identity.

Lab 2.2 — Extensibility threat and contract map
  1. Choose a hypothetical integration and define its tool schema: purpose, parameters, outputs, errors, sensitivity, and side effects.
  2. Compare connector/API, MCP, computer use, Microsoft 365 extension, and hosted-agent options; select the least fragile fit.
  3. Draw the identity path from user to host, agent, tool, and target system. Mark authorization enforcement and data exposure at every hop.
  4. Add input validation, output sanitization, confirmation, timeout, retry, audit, version, and revocation behavior.

Success check: an untrusted request cannot expand tool scope, bypass target-system authorization, or cause a consequential change without the intended approval.

Exam tip
MCP describes a tool protocol, not an authorization model. Always reason separately about who authenticates and who is permitted to perform the underlying action.

2.3 Orchestrate prebuilt agents and apps

Inventory AI features already available in the business applications before creating a parallel custom experience. Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain, Customer Experience and Service, and Microsoft 365 have evolving embedded capabilities and agents. Confirm which feature is available in the target application, release, region, and entitlement; then determine whether it can be configured or extended to meet the process. Do not infer a feature's availability from a product-family name.

For Sales and Service experiences in Microsoft 365, assess the user's daily work context, CRM/service system of record, available data connectors, and the identity under which information is surfaced. In Dynamics 365 Sales, the documented Copilot experience can support natural-language questions, record summaries, meeting preparation, emails, and account updates, but capabilities and transition paths change. Validate current documentation and the configured tenant. For Finance/SCM agent chat interoperability, identify the additional knowledge source, connection path, access model, and owner; for in-app help and guidance, curate contextual content with a lifecycle and measure whether answers resolve the user's task.

Orchestration across apps should preserve the system of record and avoid duplicate writes. If a sequence spans CRM, Finance, and a collaboration experience, define the transaction boundary, idempotency strategy, handoff state, and reconciliation path. Use common identifiers and an explicit source-of-truth map. A user-facing answer should distinguish retrieved evidence from an action result; “the request is submitted” should be based on a confirmed system response, not an agent's intent.

Architect scenario. Alpine Ski House wants one assistant to answer a seller's account question, summarize a service issue, and suggest a finance action. Route each request to the app capability or governed source that owns it. Provide a cross-app orchestration only for a demonstrated workflow; keep each write scoped to its owning application and return a clear completion status or human handoff.

Lab 2.3 — Prebuilt capability and app map

  2. For each user task, mark prebuilt, configure, extend, or build; record a Learn link and tenant validation question.
  3. Draw records, IDs, system of record, user identity, read/write path, and where the human agent takes over.
  4. Draft end-to-end cases for a successful request, stale data, denied access, duplicate event, and partial downstream failure.

Deploy AI-powered business solutions
3.1 Analyze, monitor, and tune

Monitoring should connect operational health to user and business outcomes. Define an observable chain from user request through orchestration, retrieval, model response, tool call, and final result. Capture appropriate traces and metrics for latency, failure, retries, tool success, escalation, groundedness or task quality, feedback, and process outcomes. Minimize or redact sensitive content in telemetry; set access, retention, and investigation controls before enabling detailed traces.

Review the backlog of feedback and incidents by theme: incorrect or unsupported answer, retrieval gap, stale source, missing permission, poor prompt/instruction, tool/API error, workflow design, or model behavior. Tune the cause, not just the visible response. Update evaluations and regression tests whenever prompts, data, tools, models, or business rules change. A change that improves average response quality can still worsen a high-impact edge case, so segment results by task, user group, source, and risk tier.

SignalWhat it can indicateNext investigation
Latency and timeout trendModel, retrieval, dependency, or orchestration bottleneckTrace stage-level timing; test load and dependencies before changing model.
Tool failures / retriesContract drift, permissions, bad inputs, rate or dependency issueInspect structured errors, identity, connector health, and idempotency.
Low task success / escalation increaseGrounding, instructions, routing, or use-case boundary issueSample cases; categorize evidence gaps and review fallback quality.
Feedback / backlog themesRepeated unmet need or safety/quality defectPrioritize by impact and frequency; assign an owner and testable corrective action.
Model behavior changeChanged deployment/version, routing, prompt, or input distributionCompare evaluation baselines and telemetry slices; rollback or constrain if needed.

Microsoft Foundry documentation describes tracing, monitoring, evaluations, and Application Insights integration for agent observability; Copilot Studio has platform monitoring and governance options. Instrument the relevant surfaces and correlate with business-system events using safe identifiers. Dashboards alone do not prove quality: use curated evaluation sets, production sampling, user feedback, and operational incident review together.

Architect scenario. Relecloud sees response times rise after adding a knowledge source, while complaint volume grows only for one product line. Compare retrieval latency and result quality by source/product, inspect access failures and freshness, then adjust the corpus or retrieval strategy and re-run the regression set. Avoid blindly switching all requests to a more expensive model.

Lab 3.1 — Telemetry-to-tuning loop
  1. Draw the event path from request to final business result; select metrics for latency, task quality, safety, tool outcomes, escalation, and business impact.
  2. Define a feedback taxonomy and a triage owner for the top failure categories.
  3. Draft a dashboard slice for task, model/deployment, source, user segment, and time while excluding unnecessary sensitive payloads.
  4. For a sample defect, write a hypothesis, corrective change, regression cases, rollout gate, and rollback trigger.

Success check: a support engineer can detect the issue, trace its stage, identify affected users, and verify a measured correction without exposing more content than necessary.

Exam tip
Telemetry is evidence, not a tuning instruction. First find whether the defect comes from data, access, prompt, model, tool, or workflow; then use a targeted change and regression tests.

3.2 Manage testing

Test the business task end to end, not just the model's answer in a prompt playground. A test set should represent normal requests, paraphrases, ambiguous requests, out-of-scope requests, edge cases, conflicting or missing evidence, permission-denied users, tool failures, and adversarial content. Use a mix of deterministic assertions (required fields, policy, tool invocation, authorization) and quality judgments (relevance, completeness, groundedness, tone, and usefulness). Specify acceptance thresholds by risk rather than one universal score.

For custom models, validate task performance on representative held-out data, including relevant segments and failure cases. Track provenance and permitted use of training/evaluation data, leakage risks, bias, drift, and changes between model versions. Do not use the training set as the only evidence of quality. For prompts, make the purpose explicit, supply relevant context, constrain output format when needed, and test against representative variation. Evaluate the downstream action as well as text: a plausible response can still trigger the wrong case update.

Keep a versioned set tied to solution components and release gates. Retest after changing prompts, model deployments or routing, connectors, permissions, knowledge sources, channels, or downstream business rules.

Architect scenario. Contoso's agent summarizes service history and can create a follow-up task across two Dynamics 365 apps. Test more than the summary: verify the correct customer record, user-specific visibility, duplicate submissions, failed task creation, and a handoff when the source is missing. A natural-sounding answer alone is not a pass.

Lab 3.2 — Evaluation set and release gates
  1. Write 12 synthetic cases spanning happy path, paraphrases, ambiguity, missing/stale/conflicting data, denied access, tool failure, and prompt manipulation.
  2. For every case, define the expected source, allowed tool behavior, answer rubric, and whether human escalation is required.
  3. Define separate pass thresholds for factuality/grounding, action correctness, safety, latency, and high-risk segments.
  4. Run the same cases before and after a prompt/model/data change; record differences and a rollback rule.

Success check: the test plan catches incorrect actions and unsafe behavior as well as poor prose, and every release has reproducible evidence.

Exam tip
Design at least one negative case for every action and data source. Test denied access, unavailable dependency, and ambiguous input—not only the happy path.

Did you know?

Microsoft's AB-100 outline explicitly includes building test cases using Copilot and end-to-end scenarios across multiple Dynamics 365 apps. Human review remains necessary to ensure the generated cases cover the actual acceptance criteria.

3.3 Design ALM

Application lifecycle management (ALM) is the controlled path from idea through requirements, development, testing, release, maintenance, and decommissioning. Use isolated development, test, and production environments appropriate to the organization's governance. Package Power Platform components in solutions; keep environment-specific settings in environment variables and connections in connection references. Use managed release practices, approvals, source control, and deployment automation appropriate to the toolchain.

Extend ALM to all AI dependencies: prompt and agent definitions, connectors, actions, tool schemas, model/deployment configuration, evaluation sets, grounding indexes/data pipelines, policy, and telemetry. Track versions and ownership, protect secrets, verify environment-specific access, and capture data/model changes in audit evidence. A Foundry agent may use a managed runtime or team-owned hosted code; the latter also requires code builds, dependency management, container/image security, infrastructure configuration, and endpoint rollout. Do not assume one product's solution package captures every cross-platform dependency.

DevelopLeast-privilege maker access, versioned artifacts, synthetic/test data, secret managementChange set, owner, peer review, dependency list.
ValidateAutomated checks, evaluations, security/privacy review, integration and failure testsTest results against release thresholds and documented exceptions.

Use a release manifest to state which version of each agent, prompt, tool, model, source, and evaluation set was tested. Separate promotion of code/configuration from data movement when appropriate, and validate target-environment permissions before the first user request. Define a rollback that restores a known-good version without silently reverting the underlying business data.

Architect scenario. Proseware has a Copilot Studio agent that invokes an action and a Foundry-hosted specialist agent. The Power Platform solution transports the agent-related components it supports, but the architecture also versions the Foundry artifact, API contract, deployment configuration, and evaluation set. Promotion stops if the integration test uses a production credential or if the target environment lacks an approved connection.

Lab 3.3 — Draft an AI ALM pipeline
  1. Draw development, test, and production stages and identify owners, approvals, and environment boundaries.
  2. List versioned assets: solutions, agents, prompts, actions/connectors, models, data/index configuration, test sets, and monitoring rules.
  3. Map environment variables, connection references, identities, secret storage, and who can approve production changes.
  4. Add quality/security gates, staged rollout, observability checks, rollback, incident response, and retirement steps.

Success check: a release can be reproduced and traced to tested versions, secrets are not embedded in artifacts, and rollback/retirement do not leave active identities or integrations behind.

Exam tip
Power Platform solutions and pipelines are only part of an AI ALM design. Include non-solution assets—models, grounding data, evaluation evidence, and hosted code—in the dependency inventory.

Did you know?

Microsoft's Copilot Studio ALM guidance recommends solutions for transporting components, environment variables for environment-specific settings, and connection references for environment-specific connections and credentials.

3.4 Responsible AI, security, governance, risk, and compliance

Responsible AI is an ongoing lifecycle practice. Assess the people affected, intended and prohibited uses, expected benefits, foreseeable harms, transparency needs, human accountability, and how users can report or contest outcomes. Fairness, reliability and safety, privacy and security, inclusiveness, transparency, and accountability are design and operating concerns—not a final checklist. Identify decision ownership and provide human review in proportion to impact.

For security, map the threat surface across prompts, retrieved documents, connected tools, model inputs/outputs, identities, logs, and downstream systems. Prompt injection can appear in user instructions or untrusted retrieved content; treat content as data, constrain the tool allowlist and permissions, validate tool inputs, and require confirmation or policy checks for sensitive actions. Apply least privilege to grounding data and tools; the agent should inherit or enforce the user's allowed access rather than bypass it. Protect model configuration and tuning data, track changes, and keep audit trails for data/model updates.

Governance should cover agent inventory and ownership, environment and connector policy, approved data sources, human review, incident response, retention, and change management. Microsoft's Copilot Studio security and governance guidance describes data policies, authentication, knowledge/tool controls, channel publication, monitoring, and audit options. Apply the specific controls offered by the platform and validate current scope; product-level features do not replace enterprise policy or a documented data-flow review.

Architect scenario. Tailspin's agent searches SharePoint contracts and can create a renewal task. A retrieved contract contains text instructing the agent to export all customer data. Treat that instruction as untrusted document content, not authority. Keep retrieval permission-aware, limit the agent to the renewal tool, validate the customer/contract identifier, require confirmation where appropriate, and log the bounded action. Test cross-tenant access, stale contract, prompt injection, and denied action.

Lab 3.4 — Threat-model prompt injection
  1. Draw trust boundaries for the user, agent, retrieved sources, model, tools, identity provider, and business system.
  2. List threats: malicious user prompt, hostile document, data leakage, tool misuse, identity confusion, model/data tampering, and unauthorized regional movement.
  3. For each threat, assign preventive, detective, and recovery controls; include least privilege, validation, human approval, telemetry minimization, and rollback.
  4. Create adversarial tests for prompt manipulation in both user text and retrieved content, and verify that the agent refuses unsafe tool behavior while completing legitimate tasks.
  5. Record who owns the risk decision, how users report harm, what audit evidence is retained, and how changes are approved.

Success check: untrusted content cannot grant itself authority, the agent cannot use tools beyond the approved scope, and an incident can be investigated without collecting unnecessary sensitive data.

Exam tip
Prompt injection is not solved by a stronger system prompt alone. Combine trust-boundary design with data permissions, constrained tools, validation, human approval, monitoring, and incident response.

Architecture decision frameworks

Build, buy, or extend

Start by checking whether a supported product capability already meets the need. Compare total lifecycle cost, differentiation, data/control requirements, time to value, integration effort, operational ownership, quality evidence, and portability. Extend an existing copilot or platform when its interaction model and governance are a fit. Build only when a validated gap justifies owning the additional engineering and run burden. A decision record should make assumptions and the pilot's measurable go/no-go gates explicit.

Standard NLP, CLU, or generative orchestration

ApproachBest fitTrade-off
Rules / topic designKnown flows, exact routing, policy-driven sequence.Deterministic and inspectable; can require manual coverage of variations.
CLUClassification/extraction for a bounded set of intents and entities.Focused understanding; requires representative utterance coverage and confidence handling.
Generative orchestrationVariable natural-language tasks needing tool/knowledge selection and multi-step planning.Flexible; requires strong boundaries, evaluations, and clear fallback for uncertainty.

Agent types and autonomy

Use a prompt/response agent for an explicit interaction; a task agent for a bounded task and tools; an autonomous agent for trigger-driven work only when its authority, monitoring, recovery, and human oversight are well defined. These terms describe patterns; exact product features and availability change. Keep autonomy proportional to the reversibility and impact of the action.

Extend Microsoft 365 Copilot or build a custom agent?

Extend Microsoft 365 Copilot when users need domain knowledge or actions within a supported Microsoft 365 work experience. Use Copilot Studio for configurable business agents, tools, and channels where low-code composition suits the workflow. Use Microsoft Foundry when model, code, runtime, or orchestration control is required. Before choosing, check supported extensibility, user identity, connectors/data access, governance, entitlements, and release path in the current documentation and tenant.

Paper exercise — one-page architecture choice
  1. State the business outcome and baseline.
  2. Compare four options: existing product feature, M365 Copilot extension, Copilot Studio, and Foundry/custom.
  3. Score each on capability fit, data/identity, control, cost/TCO, operations, and time to value.
  4. Recommend the least complex fit, list uncertainties to validate, and name the pilot gate.

Success check: the decision is evidence-based and includes an honest explanation of why the other options were not selected.
