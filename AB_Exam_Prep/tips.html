Tips, tricks & did you know

Practical habits for studying with limited hours, reading scenario questions the way an exam writer intends, and staying calm on exam day. Every section collapses.

Studying with about five hours a week
  • Follow the weights, not your comfort zone. AB-620 puts 40–45% on integrating and extending agents in Copilot Studio; AB-100 puts 35–40% on planning. Your plan gives those areas the most sessions on purpose.
  • Build, don’t just read. A free Copilot Studio trial or developer environment lets you create topics, add knowledge, call a connector, and publish to a test channel. One hands-on lab beats an hour of rereading.
  • Close every session with a drill. Six questions in eight minutes tells you whether the material stuck while it is still fresh.
  • Keep the error log honest. Write the rule you broke, not just the right answer. Rereading 20 rules is faster than redoing 200 questions.
  • Use the Learn practice assessment. Microsoft’s free assessment is the best guide to real wording. Take it after your first full practice exam, not on day one.
Did you know?

Microsoft publishes a study guide for each exam with the exact skills measured. When it changes, the guide shows the effective date — check it again the week you book.

Reading scenario questions
  • Find the constraint first. Phrases like “minimize administrative effort,” “without custom code,” “least privilege,” or “must stay in the tenant” decide between two otherwise correct answers.
  • Prefer the native capability. If the platform does it out of the box (a built-in connector, a managed environment setting, a DLP policy), that usually beats a custom build.
  • Watch the role in the question. AB-620 asks what the builder configures. AB-100 asks what the architect recommends — often the plan, governance, or ROI decision rather than the click path.
  • Eliminate, then choose. Strike answers that break a stated requirement. Two answers often survive; reread the constraint to choose between them.
  • Distrust absolutes. “Always” and “never” options are rarely right for a design question.
Trick

For case studies, read the questions before the case. Then scan the case for only the facts each question needs.

AB-620-specific tips
  • Know when generative orchestration selects topics and tools on its own versus when you need explicit trigger phrases or a classic topic.
  • Be able to choose between knowledge sources (SharePoint, Dataverse, public websites, uploaded files) based on freshness, permissions, and scale.
  • Understand how authentication choices affect what a user’s agent can see, and how connector credentials are scoped.
  • Practice the test-and-manage loop: test pane, conversation transcripts, analytics, solutions for ALM, and environment-level governance.

AB-100-specific tips
  • Think in business outcomes: a correct AB-100 answer usually ties a solution to a measurable goal, a responsible-AI control, and a rollout path.
  • Know when to recommend Microsoft 365 Copilot, a Copilot Studio agent, a Dynamics 365 or Power Platform capability, or Azure AI Foundry.
  • Expect governance, security, data residency, and cost trade-offs in design questions, not just features.
  • For deploy questions, look for staged rollout, monitoring, change management, and adoption measures.

Reading your practice scores
  • Practice results here are percent correct on original questions. They are not Microsoft scaled scores, and nothing converts between them.
  • Microsoft’s passing score is 700 on a 1–1,000 scale. That is not 70% correct; question weighting varies.
  • Use the per-domain breakdown. A strong total can hide one weak domain that the real exam will find.
  • The site’s 80% readiness target is a study heuristic. Reaching it does not guarantee a pass.
Exam-day strategy
  • Pace with checkpoints. Note the question count on the first screen and set halfway and three-quarter checkpoints against the clock.
  • Flag and move. Don’t spend five minutes on one item. Answer your best guess, flag it, and come back.
  • Answer everything. Unanswered items earn nothing.
  • Mind sections that lock. Some exams don’t let you return to a section (often case studies or yes/no series). Read the instructions screen.
  • Online proctoring: run the system test days ahead, clear the desk, close other apps, and check in early.
