BeginnerMicrosoft 365 Copilot Chat

Ask one question instead of opening eleven tabs.

Demo material: Attach this text file in Copilot Chat. It replaces email, Teams, and meeting data.01-Quality-Topic-Update.txt
Set upOpen Copilot Chat and attach Demo-Files/01-Quality-Topic-Update.txt. Keep Work IQ off; this demo uses only the attached fictional file.

Main prompt: This is the full four-part GCSE pattern.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Help me get back up to speed on an open quality topic I own.
Context: I work in Quality and I have been out of this thread for about two weeks. I need to know what changed, what was decided, and what is still open before my next review meeting.
Source: Use only the attached file 01-Quality-Topic-Update.txt. Do not search my email, Teams, meetings, or other files.
Expectation: Give me a summary in three labeled parts: Decisions made, Still open, and Who owns what. Keep it under 250 words, use plain language, and cite the source message or file for every point.

Follow-up: Converts prose into something you can paste straight into a meeting agenda or a tracker.

Follow-up 1
Turn the 'Still open' items into a table with these columns: Item, Owner, What is blocking it, Date last discussed, and Suggested next step. Sort by date last discussed, oldest first.

Follow-up: This is your verification habit.

Follow-up 2
For each open item, tell me what evidence you actually found versus what you inferred. If something is an assumption on your part, mark it clearly as an assumption and tell me what document would confirm it.
Save as a custom instruction: Always tell me what you found in my sources versus what you inferred, and label assumptions clearly.

Follow-up: Closes the loop.

Follow-up 3
Draft a short Teams message I can send to the group that lists the three oldest open items and asks each owner for a status by end of week. Keep it friendly, under 120 words, and do not include anything confidential beyond the item names.

BeginnerMicrosoft 365 Copilot Chat, Word, Edge

Read the 40-page document in ninety seconds, then decide if you need the other 39 pages.

Demo material: Attach the procedure text first. The guidance excerpt is used in the final comparison follow-up.02-Quality-Procedure-Change.txt · 03-External-Guidance-Excerpt.txt
Set upOpen Copilot Chat and attach Demo-Files/02-Quality-Procedure-Change.txt. Keep 03-External-Guidance-Excerpt.txt ready for the final follow-up.

Main prompt: Notice the Expectation does three jobs at once: it sets the format, it demands section-level traceability, and it asks Copilot to triage what you still need to read personally.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Summarize a long quality document so I can decide whether it needs a full review from me.
Context: I am on the Quality team and I have about ten minutes. I need to know what this document requires of my team, what is new or unusual in it, and where the risk sits — not a general overview.
Source: Use only the attached file 02-Quality-Procedure-Change.txt.
Expectation: Give me: (1) a five-bullet executive summary, (2) a list of every requirement, limit, or acceptance criterion the document places on my team with the section number, (3) the three areas I should read in full myself and why. Plain language, no jargon I would have to look up.

Follow-up: Same source, different audience, no re-reading.

Follow-up 1
Now rewrite that same summary for a line supervisor who has five minutes before shift start. Cut it to one screen, use short sentences, and lead with what changes for their team tomorrow.

Follow-up: This is the Quality-specific move.

Follow-up 2
List anything in this document that is ambiguous, internally inconsistent, or missing a numeric acceptance criterion. For each one, quote the exact sentence and explain what a reviewer would likely challenge.
Save as a custom instruction: When I review a procedure or protocol, always flag vague qualifiers and any requirement that lacks a numeric acceptance criterion.

Follow-up: Compares two supplied sources without relying on a live website or tenant content.

Follow-up 3
Now attach 03-External-Guidance-Excerpt.txt. Compare it with the procedure change: show where they agree, where they conflict, and which statements still need verification against an official published source.

BeginnerMicrosoft 365 Copilot Chat

The fastest way to get better answers is to stop writing prompts alone.

Demo material: Open this file and paste one weak prompt into the Context field.04-Weak-Prompt-Practice.txt
Set upOpen Demo-Files/04-Weak-Prompt-Practice.txt and choose one weak prompt. Paste it where the main prompt says [PASTE ONE WEAK PROMPT].

Main prompt: Asking Copilot to critique against the four named fields teaches you the framework by showing you which one you skipped.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Improve a prompt I wrote so it produces a more useful answer.
Context: I work in Quality at a global beauty manufacturer. Here is the prompt I used and the kind of answer I wanted but did not get. My prompt was: "[PASTE YOUR ORIGINAL PROMPT HERE]". What I actually wanted was [DESCRIBE THE ANSWER YOU WANTED].
Source: Use only the weak prompt and desired outcome I paste here. Do not search any files or work data.
Expectation: Do three things: (1) tell me specifically what is weak about my prompt, mapped to Goal, Context, Source, and Expectation; (2) give me a rewritten version I can copy and paste; (3) give me two variations of the rewrite for different situations. Do not run the improved prompt yet.

Follow-up: Seeing the before-and-after in the same conversation is what converts the framework from a slide into a habit.

Follow-up 1
Now run your rewritten version and show me the result. Afterwards, tell me in one line what the rewrite changed about the answer.

Follow-up: This is how one person's good prompt becomes the team's standard work.

Follow-up 2
Turn the rewritten prompt into a reusable template for my team. Replace anything specific to me with clearly marked placeholders in square brackets, and add a one-line note explaining when to use it.
Save as a custom instruction: I work in Quality at a global beauty manufacturer. Default to plain language, cite sources, flag assumptions, and never invent lot numbers, dates, or test results.

IntermediateExcel

Ask the spreadsheet what it contains instead of scrolling to row 4,000.

Demo material: Open in Excel, save a working copy to OneDrive or SharePoint, select the data, and press Ctrl+T.05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv
Set upOpen Demo-Files/05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv in Excel, save a working copy to OneDrive or SharePoint, select the data, and press Ctrl+T to make it a table.

Main prompt: This is the analysis-readiness check.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Help me understand this quality data set and find problems in the data before I analyze it.
Context: I am a Quality analyst and I did not build this workbook. Before I trust any conclusion from it, I need to know what each column means, what time period it covers, and whether the data is clean enough to analyze.
Source: Use the table on the active worksheet only. Do not pull in any other file or sheet.
Expectation: Give me: (1) a one-line plain-language description of each column and the data type it holds, (2) the date range and total record count, (3) a list of data-quality issues — blanks, duplicates, inconsistent spellings, outliers, or values that look wrong for their column. Put the data-quality issues in a table with the column name, the issue, how many rows are affected, and what I should do about it.
Save as a skill: This is a repeatable, multi-step workflow with defined inputs and outputs. Save it as a Copilot skill after your team agrees on the final steps and review gates.

Follow-up: Copilot writes the formula and inserts the column for you.

Follow-up 1
Add a new column called Aging Bucket that groups Days Open into 0-30, 31-60, 61-90, and over 90. Use a formula, not pasted values, and tell me the formula you used.
Save as a custom instruction: When you add a calculated column in Excel, always use a live formula and tell me the formula you used.

Follow-up: Conditional formatting rules survive new data; manual fill does not.

Follow-up 2
Highlight every row where Severity is Critical and Status is Open in red fill, and every row where Days Open is over 90 in amber fill. Use conditional formatting rules, not manual fill.

Follow-up: A deliberately skeptical prompt.

Follow-up 3
Which three columns in this data set would be the most misleading if someone summarized them without reading the definitions first? Explain the trap in each one.

IntermediateExcel

From raw records to the chart you actually present, without a single right-click.

Demo material: Continue in the workbook created from this CSV in Demo 4.05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv
Set upContinue in the workbook created from 05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv in Demo 4.

Main prompt: One prompt, three deliverables: a pivot for exploration, a Pareto for prioritization, and a written finding for the deck.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Analyze where our quality issues are concentrated and whether the trend is improving or worsening.
Context: I am preparing the monthly Quality review. Leadership wants to know where to put resources next quarter, so concentration and direction of travel matter more than totals.
Source: Use only the table on this worksheet.
Expectation: Do three things: (1) build a PivotTable summarizing record count and total units affected by Root Cause Category and by Site, (2) create a Pareto-style view showing which root cause categories account for the top 80 percent of units affected, (3) tell me in three sentences whether the trend over the covered months is improving, worsening, or flat, and what specific evidence supports that.
Save as a skill: This is a repeatable, multi-step workflow with defined inputs and outputs. Save it as a Copilot skill after your team agrees on the final steps and review gates.

Follow-up: Adds the time dimension, which is where most root-cause insight actually lives.

Follow-up 1
Now show the same analysis split by month so I can see whether the concentration is shifting. Add a line chart of critical and major records per month, and tell me which month broke the pattern and what is different about it.

Follow-up: The single most important Excel follow-up in a regulated environment.

Follow-up 2
Challenge your own conclusion. What would have to be true for the trend you just described to be wrong? Point to specific columns, sample sizes, or gaps in this data that could be driving a false signal.
Save as a custom instruction: Whenever you give me a trend or conclusion from data, also tell me what would have to be true for it to be wrong.

Follow-up: Turns the analysis into the sentence you will actually say out loud.

Follow-up 3
Write a three-sentence finding statement for my Quality review, in plain language, with the specific numbers included. Then write a second version aimed at a plant operations audience who cares about what changes on the floor.

IntermediateExcel — Edit with Copilot

The ten-step cleanup you have done a hundred times, described once.

Demo material: Continue on a copy of the Demo 4 workbook.05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv
Set upMake a copy of the workbook created from 05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv, then open Edit with Copilot.

Main prompt: This is a full standard-work cleanup expressed as one instruction.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Clean and standardize this quality data set so it is ready for reporting, in one pass.
Context: I am working on a copy of the workbook, so you can make changes directly. This data comes from more than one site and the formatting is inconsistent. I need it reporting-ready, and I need to be able to explain every change I made.
Source: Use only the table on the active worksheet.
Expectation: Do all of the following and show me your plan before you execute: trim leading and trailing spaces in every text column; standardize Site and Category values to consistent capitalization; convert any date stored as text into a real date; flag duplicate Record IDs rather than deleting them; freeze the header row; auto-fit columns; and add a summary sheet listing every change you made and how many cells it affected.
Save as a custom instruction: When you edit my workbook, always show your plan first, never delete rows without asking, and log every change on a summary sheet.
Save as a skill: This is a repeatable, multi-step workflow with defined inputs and outputs. Save it as a Copilot skill after your team agrees on the final steps and review gates.

Follow-up: Percentiles and standard deviation are exactly the numbers a Quality review needs and exactly the ones people skip because the Excel formulas are fiddly.

Follow-up 1
Use Python to run a proper statistical summary of Days Open by Severity — mean, median, standard deviation, and the 90th percentile for each severity level — and put the results in a clearly labeled table on a new sheet.

Follow-up: Moves from descriptive statistics to statistical process control — the framing Quality teams actually use to decide whether something is signal or noise.

Follow-up 2
Create a control-chart style view of critical and major records per month, with the mean and the upper control limit at mean plus three standard deviations drawn as lines. Tell me if any month is outside the limit.

Follow-up: Practicing the undo deliberately is what makes people brave enough to use agentic editing on real work.

Follow-up 3
Undo everything you just did and tell me what you removed, so I can confirm the workbook is back to its original state.

IntermediatePowerPoint

You already did the work. Stop rebuilding it as slides.

Demo material: Use this fictional investigation brief as the PowerPoint source.06-Packaging-Investigation-Brief.pdf
Set upOpen PowerPoint with a blank presentation or your approved template, then use Demo-Files/06-Packaging-Investigation-Brief.pdf as the source.

Main prompt: Every clause in the Expectation is a decision you would otherwise make by hand later: slide count, narrative arc, reading level, visual treatment, and the discipline of pushing detail into notes instead of onto slides.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Create a review presentation from a document I have already written.
Context: I am presenting to a cross-functional Quality review with plant, supply chain, and regulatory attendees. They are smart but not all Quality specialists, and I have a fifteen-minute slot including questions.
Source: Build it only from the attached file 06-Packaging-Investigation-Brief.pdf.
Expectation: Ten slides maximum. Structure it as: what happened, what the data shows, root cause, what we are doing about it, what we need from this group. Plain language over jargon. Use a clean, restrained visual style with no stock photography. Put the detail in the speaker notes rather than on the slides.
Save as a custom instruction: When you build a deck for me, default to ten slides or fewer, plain language, no stock photography, and detail in the speaker notes.
Save as a skill: This is a repeatable, multi-step workflow with defined inputs and outputs. Save it as a Copilot skill after your team agrees on the final steps and review gates.

Follow-up: Targeted slide-level surgery.

Follow-up 1
Slide 4 is too dense. Split it into two slides, turn the bullet list into a simple comparison table, and move everything that is explanation rather than evidence into the speaker notes.

Follow-up: The anticipated-question line is the highest-value part.

Follow-up 2
Write speaker notes for every slide, in my voice, at about 45 seconds of spoken content per slide. For each slide also add the one question this audience is most likely to ask and a one-line answer.

Follow-up: A red-team pass before you present.

Follow-up 3
Review this deck as if you were a skeptical auditor. Which claims are not supported by the source document, which numbers lack context, and where would you ask for evidence?
Save as a custom instruction: Before I present anything, review it as a skeptical auditor and flag claims that are not supported by my source.

AdvancedPowerPoint

Make the deck yours, then make next month's version take five minutes.

Demo material: Continue with the deck generated in Demo 7; no live data is needed.No file needed
Set upKeep the deck generated from 06-Packaging-Investigation-Brief.pdf in Demo 7 open. It is both the content source and the starting point for this exercise.

Main prompt: The 'flag rather than invent filler' clause is the whole safety mechanism.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Rebuild this deck so it matches the structure and style of a presentation my team already trusts.
Context: My team has a monthly Quality review deck with a format leadership is used to. Consistency month over month matters more to this audience than novelty, and rebuilding the structure by hand every month is wasted effort.
Source: Use the current presentation as the source. Preserve its facts and structure; do not search work data or invent missing content.
Expectation: Keep the section order, slide types, and visual language of the existing deck. Replace the content with mine. Flag any section in the pattern deck that I have no content for, rather than inventing filler for it.
Save as a custom instruction: When reusing a deck as a pattern, never invent content for a section I have no data for — flag the gap instead.
Save as a skill: This is a repeatable, multi-step workflow with defined inputs and outputs. Save it as a Copilot skill after your team agrees on the final steps and review gates.

Follow-up: Long and short versions of the same deck in one file is a standard Quality reviewer need, and it is a two-sentence instruction rather than a Saturday afternoon.

Follow-up 1
Cut this to a five-slide version for an executive audience that only needs the decision and the risk. Keep the full version intact as separate slides at the end so I still have the backup detail.

Follow-up: Practical for a global Quality organization.

Follow-up 2
Translate the five-slide executive version into [target language], keeping the technical quality terms in English where a translation would be ambiguous. Tell me which terms you left in English and why.

Follow-up: The open and the close are what determine whether a Quality review produces a decision or another meeting.

Follow-up 3
I am about to present this in Teams. Give me a 60-second opening that frames why this matters to a mixed plant, supply chain, and regulatory audience, and three closing questions that will get a decision rather than a discussion.

AdvancedExcel → PowerPoint → Outlook / Teams

This is the demo that changes how Monday feels.

Demo material: Use the workbook from Demos 4–6 and the deck from Demos 7–8.05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv · 06-Packaging-Investigation-Brief.pdf
Set upUse the workbook created from 05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv and the deck created from 06-Packaging-Investigation-Brief.pdf. No email, Teams, or tenant data is required.

Main prompt: One prompt, four artifacts, one source.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Take me from my quality data set to a review-ready package in one pass.
Context: I am preparing for a monthly Quality review with plant, supply chain, and regulatory stakeholders. I have the data analysis done and I need the communication layer. Everything must be traceable back to the data — I will be asked where every number came from.
Source: Use only the workbook created from 05-Fictional-Nonconformance-Data.csv and the current deck created from 06-Packaging-Investigation-Brief.pdf.
Expectation: Produce four things: (1) a one-paragraph executive summary with the three numbers that matter most, (2) an action table with Action, Owner, Due date, and the evidence that justifies it, (3) a short stakeholder email suitable for the review invite, (4) a list of the questions this audience is most likely to ask, with where in my data the answer lives. Do not invent owners or dates — use placeholders where I have to fill them in.
Save as a custom instruction: Never invent owners, due dates, lot numbers, or test results. Use clearly marked placeholders instead.
Save as a skill: This is a repeatable, multi-step workflow with defined inputs and outputs. Save it as a Copilot skill after your team agrees on the final steps and review gates.

Follow-up: The traceability checkpoint.

Follow-up 1
For every number in the executive summary, tell me exactly which column and which rows in the workbook it came from, and re-check the arithmetic. If any number cannot be traced, say so plainly.
Save as a custom instruction: Before I send or present anything, trace every number back to its source column and rows and re-check the arithmetic.

Follow-up: A realistic communication constraint.

Follow-up 2
Rewrite the stakeholder email for someone who will read it on a phone between meetings. Lead with the decision I need from them, keep it under 100 words, and put the detail in a linked or attached summary rather than in the body.

Follow-up: Pre-reads are the thing everyone agrees they should send and nobody has time to write.

Follow-up 3
Build me a one-page pre-read that someone could read in three minutes and arrive at the review already informed. Structure it as: the situation, what the data shows, the decision required, and the risk of doing nothing.

AdvancedMicrosoft 365 Copilot — Memory, Custom Instructions, Prompt Gallery

Teach Copilot who you are once, instead of explaining it every morning.

Demo material: No source file is needed. Use the save callouts in this guide.No file needed
Set upOpen Copilot Chat settings and use the custom-instruction and skill callouts collected in this guide.

Main prompt: Custom instructions are the highest-return five minutes in Copilot and almost nobody sets them.

First prompt · GCSE
Goal: Help me write a set of custom instructions that make Copilot useful for my Quality role by default.
Context: I work in Quality at a global beauty manufacturer. I work mostly in Excel and PowerPoint, I present to mixed technical and non-technical audiences, and I operate in a regulated environment where an unverified claim is a real problem, not a small one.
Source: Use what I have told you here plus the instruction notes I paste from this guide. Do not search my work data.
Expectation: Give me a set of five to eight custom instructions, each one sentence, written as direct standing instructions to Copilot. Cover how I want answers formatted, how I want sources cited, how I want assumptions flagged, and what you must never invent. Then explain in one line why each one earns its place.

Follow-up: Role-level and task-level instructions serve different purposes.

Follow-up 1
Now write a second set of instructions specifically for when I am working in Excel, covering how I want formulas, charts, and data-quality issues handled.

Follow-up: This is the adoption step.

Follow-up 2
Take the three prompts I used most successfully today and turn each one into a shareable team template, with bracketed placeholders and a one-line description of when to use it. Format them so I can paste them straight into Prompt Gallery.

Follow-up: The most credible way to close a Copilot session is with the boundaries.

Follow-up 3
What should I deliberately not use Copilot for in my Quality role? Be specific and blunt, and explain the risk in each case rather than giving me a generic disclaimer.
Save as a custom instruction: Never present a regulatory interpretation, a disposition decision, or a validated calculation as final without qualified human review.
